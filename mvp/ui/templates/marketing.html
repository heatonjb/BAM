The problem

Dashboards tell you what happened. They don't tell you what to change.

Every analytics tool shows the same thing: a metric moved. Almost none of them can say which node to touch, what it would cost, what it would break, and whether the result would survive contact with your cashflow. The gap isn't data. It's the model.

01 / FLAT

Numbers without structure

Revenue is a line in a report. It's actually the endpoint of a chain — units sold, price paid, customers acquired, staff scheduled, stock ordered. Collapse the chain and you lose the only thing you can act on.

02 / BLIND

No reference distribution

"Gross margin fell 2 points" is meaningless without knowing what normal looks like for your business type. Benchmarks have to attach to nodes, not to a dashboard footer.

03 / RECKLESS

Advice with no envelope

An agent that says "cut prices 10%" without knowing your cash runway, legal obligations, brand position or stated values is not a strategy advisor. It's a random number generator.

The model

One graph. Seven layers.

BAM projects a single underlying graph through seven layers. Transactions sit at the bottom; strategy sits at the top. Each layer is a view of the same machine, joined by typed cross-layer edges — so a change at the top can be traced down to a ledger entry, and a ledger entry can be traced up to the decision that caused it.

No off-the-shelf ontology covers all seven. BAM combines REA transaction and duality semantics, ArchiMate structure and goals, BPMN executable flow, and XBRL/FIBO statement and instrument types — then adds the connective layer those standards all leave out.

Each layer carries its own metric tree and its own benchmark population.

The algebra

The model has to conserve. We checked.

A graph that doesn't reconcile is a drawing. BAM's transformation algebra is a constraint system, and we tested it against an object-centric inventory log with a synthetic cost layer. The absorption variance isn't a fudge factor — it's internal to the identity. Omit it and the residual equals the variance you dropped. Truncate the opening balance and the snapshot breaks by exactly the predicted amount. That's a conservation proof, not a demo.

reconciliation · pinned inventory log 
# conservation identity
NI_GAAP  =  NP_TA  +  ΔInventory × overhead/unit

# executed against the pinned log
residual   =  0.0000        ✓ identity closes

# falsification checks
omit variance      →  residual = absorption variance   ✓ predicted
flat inventory     →  NI_GAAP = NP_TA                  ✓ predicted
truncated opening  →  snapshot broken by known amount  ✓ predicted

The mechanics of an exact close are cheap. Economic realism is not included in that cheapness.

The agents

Five specialists. One arbiter. A hard constraint envelope.

Agents work the graph bottom-up. Each specialist reads only its own slice — revenue, cost, marketing, operations, staffing — and returns ranked recommendations with an effort estimate and an expected effect. The arbiter then does the part that matters: it validates every recommendation against the constraint checker and selects a portfolio that maximises risk-adjusted value inside an effort budget. It decides. It does not execute.

Root agent · strategy arbiter

Portfolio selection under constraints

Every candidate initiative is checked against cashflow, legal, mission, values and brand constraints before it can enter the portfolio. Rejected ideas come back with the reason, not silence. The arbiter writes a strategic narrative explaining why this portfolio and not another — and the orchestrator executes only what a human approves.

CONSTRAINT

Cashflow

An initiative that improves margin but breaks the runway is not an improvement. Every recommendation is stress-tested against the cash position before it is ranked.

CONSTRAINT

Legal & obligations

Contracts, notice periods, regulatory duties and tax treatment are modelled as hard edges. Hard constraints cannot be overridden by an optimistic forecast.

CONSTRAINT

Mission, values, brand

These are modelled explicitly, not left to a prompt. A discount strategy that contradicts a stated brand position is rejected even when the maths works.

The research

Built from a research programme, not a pitch deck.

BAM started as a research question: can you model any business as a transformation machine precisely enough for an agent to optimise it without violating the constraints that keep it alive? The answer took 36 working papers — ontology surveys, a formal transformation algebra, a ledger prototype with five fail-closed invariants in 39 lines, an executed reconciliation, benchmark-source licensing analysis, and a survey of agent harness design and safe action-taking.

The cost shape is measured, not estimated. A thin semantic layer runs one to three person-months. A multi-entity build runs four to nine. A full ontology runs nine to eighteen with a high chance of abandonment — which is exactly why BAM starts with a minimal viable graph and grows it.

Questions

What people ask before signing up.

Is this another AI CFO?
No. AI CFO tools read your ledger and draft financial recommendations. BAM models the whole machine — operations, marketing, staffing, capacity, brand — and treats the financial statements as one projection of it. Finance is where the model is checked, not where it stops.
What is an "arbiter run" and why is it the thing you meter?
An arbiter run is one complete strategy cycle: specialists assess their slices, recommendations are validated against your constraints, and the arbiter selects a portfolio with a written rationale. It's the most expensive operation in the system because it's the one that actually thinks. Browsing the graph and drilling into nodes doesn't consume a run.
What happens when I hit the free limit?
The API returns a quota error and the UI offers an upgrade. Nothing silently degrades and no data is lost — your graph, history and initiatives all stay. You keep read access to everything you've built.
Will it change things in my systems without asking?
No. The arbiter decides; the orchestrator executes only after human approval. Write actions are routed through the same connector layer as reads and are logged with who approved what and when.
How accurate is the model if I don't connect anything?
It's structurally right and numerically indicative. A starter graph gives you the shape of your business and lets the arbiter reason about levers. Connect Xero or Stripe and the values become real. Nodes BAM can't source are explicitly marked as gaps rather than filled with plausible fiction.
Do you train models on my financial data?
No. Your tenant's data is isolated at the storage layer, and model calls carry structured summaries rather than raw documents or credentials. Benchmarks are aggregate and de-identified; we never expose one business's figures to another.
Can I export my model?
Yes. The graph is data — nodes, edges, metrics and provenance. Export as JSON or CSV at any time, on any tier. If you leave, you leave with the model.
What's the difference between Pro and Scale?
Pro is for one operator running up to five businesses with the full autonomous loop. Scale is for portfolios and franchise networks: white-label branding, custom business-type templates so every site inherits the parent's structure, SSO, and portfolio-level benchmarking.
