# Benchmarking the Middle Layers: Bootstrapping Capability, Process, and Unit-Economics Benchmarks from Public Data

## 1. The Benchmark Asymmetry: Well-Instrumented Financials, Thin Operations

In a transformation-machine model of the firm — assets and liabilities converted into outcomes through layered activities → processes → capabilities → financial statements — the bottom and top layers are densely instrumented. Every registrant's transactions roll up into XBRL-tagged financial statements with a mandated taxonomy; strategy is benchmarked through valuation multiples and peer comps. The middle layers (capability, process, unit economics) are thin. This document assesses what can and cannot be bootstrapped from public data, quantifies the comparability taxes that erode benchmarks built on XBRL, and proposes a layer-aware benchmarking methodology with validation against first-party series.

The central finding: capability/process/unit-economics proxies are derivable from public filings, but with hard ceilings. Revenue-per-employee, gross-margin decomposition, S&M intensity, and working-capital cycles can be computed at scale for ~5,000+ US filers with known, quantifiable comparability erosion (13–21% of tags are company-created extensions). True unit economics, capability maturity, and transformation yields are not publicly obtainable in audited form.

## 2. Public Data Sources That Bootstrap the Middle Layers

### 2.1 SEC EDGAR structured data — the workhorse

The SEC publishes a family of keyless JSON APIs under `data.sec.gov`, plus bulk archives:

- **Frames API** — `GET /api/xbrl/frames/{taxonomy}/{tag}/{unit}/{period}.json`. Returns one fact per reporting entity for a concept/period (e.g., `us-gaap/RevenuesFromContractWithCustomerExcludingAssessedTax/USD/CY2025.json`), each carrying CIK, entity name, accession number, and value, plus a `pts` payload count. Periods are calendarized (`CY2025`, `CY2025Q1`, `CY2025Q4I` for instantaneous). This is the most useful endpoint for cross-company benchmarks: one call returns the full distribution. [T2] SEC, "EDGAR Application Programming Interfaces," Endpoint `data.sec.gov/api/xbrl/frames/`; OpenAPI spec, Frames API paths section.
- **Company Facts API** — `GET /api/xbrl/companyfacts/CIK##########.json`; all of a company's XBRL facts, organized by taxonomy → concept → unit. **Company Concept API** — `GET /api/xbrl/companyconcept/CIK{cik}/{taxonomy}/{concept}.json`. [T2] SEC, "EDGAR Application Programming Interfaces," Endpoints `companyfacts/` and `companyconcept/`, params section.
- **Bulk archives** — `https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/daily-index/xbrl/companyfacts.zip` ("contains all the data from the XBRL Frame API and the XBRL Company Facts API") and `submissions.zip` (filing history). [T2] SEC, "EDGAR Application Programming Interfaces," Bulk download section.
- **Financial Statement Data Sets** — quarterly ZIPs (e.g., `financial-statement-data-sets/2024q1.zip`) with face-of-the-financials only, in a flattened `SUB`/`TAG`/`NUM`/`PRE` layout (a richer notes variant adds `DIM`, `TXT`, `REN`, `CAL`); since the Dec 2024 reprocessing, NUM is rendered from the primary statements as displayed, and SUB carries the registrant's SIC. Coverage runs 2009 Q1–2026 Q1, refreshed quarterly. [T2] SEC, "Financial Statement Data Sets," Overview and Scope sections.
- **Full-text and entity search** — `https://efts.sec.gov/LATEST/search-index` (Elasticsearch-style full text over filings since 2001, filterable by form type, date, entity) and sec.gov/edgar/search. This is the escape hatch for narrative-only operational data — employee counts (Item 1 Human Capital), customer counts, churn, unit-economics commentary in MD&A — which XBRL does not capture. [T2] SEC, "EDGAR Full Text Search," Full-text endpoint and form filter sections.

The Document and Entity Information (DEI) taxonomy adds covariates for peer matching: entity legal name, filer status (large accelerated / accelerated / non-accelerated / smaller reporting company), public float, and state of incorporation are tagged in the `dei` namespace. [T2] SEC Frames OpenAPI spec, taxonomies enum (`us-gaap, ifrs-full, dei, srt`).

### 2.2 Companies House — UK small-company coverage with quality caveats

For UK entities, the Public Data API (`GET /company/{company_number}` for company profiles; `GET /company/{company_number}/filing-history`) delivers registry metadata: incorporation/dissolution dates, `sic_codes`, jurisdiction, status, and filings. The **Accounts Data Product** (free daily/monthly ZIPs of iXBRL instance documents) provides tagged balance-sheet data — the CODEx programme reported 88% of the 3.1 million accounts published annually are iXBRL, but flagged "quality and consistency issues" in the tagged data. [T2] Companies House, "Companies House data products," Accounts data product section; GOV.UK CODEx Data Model document, "Usability" section. The API exposes only summary fields (accounting reference date, last/next accounts, overdue flags) rather than P&L numbers — the detail lives in iXBRL documents and PDFs, requiring a separate parsing pipeline. Electronically filed accounts cover only ~75% of the 2.2 million accounts expected per year, and UK small-company filings legally carry far less income-statement detail than US 10-Ks. [T2] Companies House, "Companies House data products," Accounts data product section, para 2.

### 2.3 Damodaran datasets — pre-aggregated US sector benchmarks

Aswath Damodaran's free yearly spreadsheets (`pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/.../datafile/margin.xls`, `wacc.xls`, `mgnroc.xls`, `Employee.html`) are compiled from ~47,810 listed companies in the 2025 edition, consolidated into 94 proprietary industry groupings built from 1990s Value Line groups. Their direct use as middle-layer benchmarks: `margin.xls` reports Gross Margin, Net Margin, EBITDA/Sales, **COGS/Sales, R&D/Sales, SG&A/Sales, Stock-Based Compensation/Sales**, and lease-adjusted operating margins by industry; `Employee.html` reports **revenues per employee and market cap per employee by industry**; `wacc.xls` gives cost of capital. Damodaran uses an *aggregated* estimator (sum of numerators ÷ sum of denominators) rather than the mean of ratios, explicitly to avoid losing money-losing firms and outlier skew; company-level data is not shareable under his provider license, and the 94 groupings are broader than SIC/NAICS sectors. [T2] Damodaran, "Data Update 1 for 2025," Section "The industry groupings"; "Operating and Net Margins," Table columns; [T3] Damodaran blog, "Data Update 1 for 2025: Draw and the danger points," https://aswathdamodaran.blogspot.com/2025/01/data-update-1-for-2025-draw-and-danger.html

### 2.4 DORA — the only frequently-published process benchmark

The Accelerate State of DevOps report (Google, DORA) is the most-cited public *process-level* benchmark. The 2024 report (10th edition; 39,000+ respondents over a decade) publishes performance clusters for the four keys: elite performers deploy ~182× more frequently with ~8× lower change-failure rate, ~127× faster change lead time, and ~2,293× faster failed-deployment recovery than low performers; 2024 added a fifth metric (deployment rework rate) and split the keys into throughput vs. stability factors. [T1] Google DORA Team, "Accelerate State of DevOps Report 2024," Performance clusters section, p. 14. The findings are survey-based: respondents self-select and self-report, samples skew toward software-enabled firms, and cluster partitions are defined from respondent data each year (2024's medium cluster even had a lower change-failure rate than high). [T3] Fenton, "The 2024 DevOps Performance Clusters," https://octopus.com/blog/2024-devops-performance-clusters

### 2.5 Specialised schemas and SaaS benchmark reports

- **SaaS/benchmark surveys** (Benchmarkit, SaaSCan, High Alpha/OpenView, KeyBanc). The 2024 reports cover ~800–1,000 companies and publish medians/quartiles for subscription gross margin (~79–80%), NRR (private median ~101%, down from ~105% in 2021; public SaaS ~110% in 2023, down from ~120% in 2022), CAC payback (5–20 months by ARR band), S&M as % of ARR (24–40%), R&D (30–53%), and ARR per FTE. [T1]/[T2] Benchmarkit, "2024 SaaS Performance Metrics Benchmark Report," Metrics section; High Alpha/OpenView, "2024 SaaS Benchmarks Report," Benchmarks table. These are voluntary self-reports — no external audit, survivorship and selection bias, and CAC conventions differ from public-company derivations (public CAC payback uses net-new ARR because contracted ARR is not disclosed). [T3] Benchmarkit, "2024 SaaS Performance Metrics" benchmarks page, https://www.benchmarkit.ai/2024benchmarks
- **Working-capital index** (J.P. Morgan, S&P 1500) — quarterly DSO/DIO/DPO/CCC aggregates: in 2023 CCC lengthened 2.4 days, DIO +3.7d, DSO +1.4d, DPO +2.7d; semiconductor CCC +28d, pharma +7.3d; ~$707B of working capital estimated releasable via next-industry-quartile matching. [T1] J.P. Morgan, "Working Capital Index 2024," Key findings section.
- **CMMI/SOC/ISO performance data** — the CMMI Institute's "Performance Results" aggregates self-reported, appraisal-corroborated objectives (24,246 appraisals, 83,379 objectives, 2020–2025; ~92% "goal achievement"; Maturity Level 3 in 75% of appraisals, Level 5 in 24%) — the sum total of public capability-maturity benchmarking, and it is appraisal-population data, not market data. [T2] CMMI Institute, "CMMI Technical Report — Performance Results," Executive summary and Appraisals by Domain sections.

### 2.6 Census/BLS sector aggregates

US BLS and Census publish industry-level productivity and inventory-to-sales ratios (e.g., Census "Manufacturing and Trade Inventories and Sales," which reports months of inventory at sales pace by NAICS category). These provide population denominators (all establishments) as opposed to the listed-company-only coverage of EDGAR/Damodaran. [T2] U.S. BLS, "SIC to NAICS 2002 Employment Ratios," table columns; U.S. Census, MTIS release, Retail categories table.

## 3. Comparability Erosion: Quantifying What a Benchmark Is Actually Measuring

Every public-data benchmark inherits taxonomy-fabrication decisions by filers. The numbers that matter:

**Extension (custom tag) rates.** The SEC's DERA trend analysis of 10-K/10-KA filings reports the average custom-tag rate declined from ~18% (2023) to ~17% (2024), with accelerated and large-accelerated filers lowest at 16%. [T2] SEC DERA, "U.S. GAAP – XBRL Custom Tags Trend 2022–2024," Trend Analysis section; [T3] XBRL US, "SEC Custom Tag Analysis Shows Slight Decline for All Filers," https://xbrl.us/news/sec-custom-tag-analysis-2025/ The SEC has stated it "continues to observe that, on average, one in every five financial items in 10-K filings is tagged with custom tags." [T1] Y. Jin et al., "Excessive custom XBRL tag usage in 10-K filings and SEC oversight," ScienceDirect (2025), Introduction, para 2. The CPA Journal's independent series (48,079 10-K/As, 151,383 10-Q/As retrieved Oct 2018) found extension use averaged ~21% of annual-report tags, rising from 9% (2009) to 25% (2013), then stabilizing ~21%; quarterly filings ~18%; heaviest extension sectors were tobacco (30%), utilities (29%), real estate (26%), insurance and financial trading (25%); lightest were measuring/control equipment (16%). [T1] Ling & Liu, "XBRL Data Comparability," The CPA Journal (2019), "Analysis" section, Exhibits 2–4. The 2025 academic study measured a mean of 13.5% custom tags per 10-K from 2009–2022, ranging from 1.9% to 42.7% across firms, and documents that the custom ratio drops significantly *after* a firm receives an SEC comment letter — evidence that a material share of extensions is misuse. [T1] Jin et al., "Excessive custom XBRL tag usage," Descriptive statistics section, Table 2, para 3. The SEC/Deloitte 2013 assessment found 96% of filers with >50% custom-tag rates were smaller companies, with rates ~2× larger filers', and 64% of high-rate smaller filers used the same three third-party providers — i.e., extension rates are partly an artifact of reporting software. [T1] SEC DERA staff observations, "Staff Observations of Custom Tag Rates," Assessment section, paras 4–6.

**Effect on cross-company comparability.** Zhu & Wu, analysing all annual XBRL filings on EDGAR as of Feb 26, 2010, found "on average, 63% of data elements are not comparable between a pair of statements." [T1] Zhu & Wu, "Interoperability of XBRL Financial Statements in the U.S.," Abstract, para 1. A 2015 study of the mandate found financial-statement *comparability* (De Franco et al. measure) actually declined post-mandate, that extension-taxonomy use is negatively associated with comparability (extensions were 24% of all tags), and that erosion concentrated in discretionary line items: SG&A comparability fell while depreciation did not change. [T1] "Effects of the SEC's XBRL mandate," Results section, paras 2–4. Debreceny et al. (2011) hand-checked extensions filed 2009–2010 and classified 40% as unnecessary (a semantically equivalent standard tag existed), 21% as aggregations/disaggregations, and 30% as new concepts — meaning ~60%+ of extensions could, in principle, have been standard. [T1] Debreceny, Farewell, Piechocki, Felden, Gräning, D'Eri, "Flex or Break? Extensions in XBRL Disclosures to the SEC," Accounting Horizons 25(4), Synopsis, para 3.

**Restatements as benchmark noise.** Ideagen Audit Analytics counted 430 restatements in 2023 (−6% from 458 in 2022; 401 companies), with Big-R (reissuance) restatements at 209 (52%); the PCAOB puts "Big R" at ~3% of filers per year 2005–2024, with ~29% of Big-R filers having changed auditors in the prior year (vs. 11% baseline). [T1] Ideagen Audit Analytics, "Financial Restatements, 2023," Findings; CAQ, "Financial Restatement Trends 2013–2022," Summary; PCAOB OERA, "Data Point: Financial Restatements and Auditor Turnover," 2025, Findings. Since companyfacts and FSDS are "as filed," a benchmark window that ignores restatements mixes error-ridden and corrected vintages.

**Taxonomy migration.** The US-GAAP taxonomy is rebuilt yearly: the 2026 GRT added 120 elements (22 from ASU/SEC rules) and deprecated 564, ahead of ~219 additions in 2025. FASB's policy keeps element *names* "unchanged, so they do not adversely affect year-to-year comparability," and since 2014 deprecated elements are never undeprecated but remain usable for two annual updates; the SEC requires referenced standard taxonomies to share a version year. [T2] FASB, "GAAP Financial Reporting Taxonomy Release Notes, 2026," Sections 1 and Appendix tables; SEC, "Release Notes for SEC Taxonomies 2026," Section 1.2. Practical impact: time-series crossing a deprecation boundary must remap via `_dep` entry points, and the Data Quality Committee has flagged "instances where registrants use older versions" of the taxonomy. [T1] XBRL US Data Quality Committee, joint meeting with SEC staff (2016 minutes), "Discussion document," para 3.

**Classification disagreement (SIC ↔ NAICS ↔ vendor codes).** Kahle & Walkling found, over ~10,000 firms jointly in Compustat and CRSP, more than 36% of classifications disagree at the 2-digit SIC level and nearly 80% at the 4-digit level; 1994 JAE work reported the same pattern. [T1] Kahle & Walkling, "The Impact of Industry Classifications on Financial Research," JFQA 31(3), Abstract, para 1. SIC↔NAICS concordances fragment massively — an analysis of 1,004 legacy SIC codes vs. 1,012 NAICS 2022 industries found 44.2% split into multiple NAICS industries (SIC 7389 alone fans into 44 NAICS codes), and BLS estimated 2017–2022 NAICS split/revision changes affected 92,500 establishments and 1.1M jobs. [T3] EntityCodes, "The SIC-to-NAICS Translation Gap," Research findings, https://entitycodes.org/research/sic-naics-translation-gap/ ; [T2] US Census concordance files via BLS, latest-vintage workbook, changes file. SEC EDGAR's SIC field is the registrant's self-asserted primary-business classification — a single code over diversified activities.

## 4. Methodology: Constructing Layer-Aware Peer Benchmarks

**Peer matching.** Because a single industry code is unreliable, score peers on a tuple: (i) primary code (SEC SIC from SUB; DEI entity data), (ii) a crosswalked alternate (weight SIC→NAICS via official Census concordances and employment/payroll weights, mapping one-to-many splits conservatively), (iii) size bands (XBRL filer status + revenue decile), (iv) age (incorporation date from Companies House for UK; DEI fields for US), and (v) geography (entity address tags; partition US vs. foreign/IFRS filers, since IFRS filers use `ifrs-full` tags). Given 2-digit SIC agreement of only ~64%, require consensus across at least two independent code sources before admitting a firm to a peer group, and treat code-mediated groups as fuzzy.

**Metric normalisation.** Compute ratios only at matched-concept level: revenue should prefer `RevenueFromContractWithCustomerExcludingAssessedTax` with a documented fallback chain (`Revenues`, `SalesRevenueNet`), never summed across aliases. Use *aggregated* estimators (sum of numerators ÷ sum of denominators, following Damodaran) for industry statistics and report median-of-firm-ratios alongside; winsorize at ~1st/99th percentile; floor sample sizes (n ≥ 30 firms, or >10 with explicit flagging). Compare like-with-like periods: the frames API calendarizes (CY vs. fiscal), so fiscal-year-end filers must be mapped to periods or kept as a separate subsample — the SEC warns that "different reporting start and end dates for facts contained in a frame" exist. [T2] SEC, "EDGAR Application Programming Interfaces," XBRL Periods section. For working-capital ratios, prefer COGS-denominated DIO/DPO but note many public datasets use sales denominators (a sales-basis turn of 7 ≈ COGS-basis 4–5 at 35–40% gross margin); [T3] Onramp Funds, "Inventory Turnover Benchmarks by Industry (2026)," Basis section, https://www.onrampfunds.com/resources/inventory-turnover-benchmarks-by-industry and prefer start-and-end average balances for inventory and receivables where multi-year fact histories exist.

**Confidence intervals.** Publish P25/P50/P75 plus a bootstrap CI (re-sample firms with replacement, 10,000 draws) rather than assuming normality, since margin, CAC-proxy, and growth distributions are skewed and bimodal by cohort. Report the *effective* n per metric separately — DSO, DPO, and DIO have different populations because inventory/payables disclosure is not universal (in one build, DSO n=591, DPO n=376, DIO n=373, CCC n=253 from the same filings). [T3] Ataski, "Small Company Financial Benchmarks by Industry 2026," Methodology/table notes, https://ataski.com/research/small-company-financial-benchmarks-2026

**Validation against first-party series.** A benchmark is only credible when it reproduces a trusted internal series: (1) *Held-out company checks* — compute a firm's ratio from XBRL and compare against the firm's own disclosed KPI (verify DSO/CCC against any firm-presented working-capital figure, or revenue-per-employee against headcount disclosure); (2) *Calibration anchors* — where firms voluntarily disclose true unit economics (HIMS reported CAC payback <12 months and LTV:CAC >4x; FIGS reported payback <6 months and LTV:CAC >3x in 2024 investor materials [T3] Putra, "Public DTC unit economics 2026," Disclosure-quality section, https://putraco.com/blog/public-dtc-unit-economics-2026-cac-gm-contribution/), use them to bound surrogate-shape parameters and size the expected *gap* between surrogates and truth; (3) *Restatement-aware backtesting* — exclude the ~3%-per-year Big-R restatements [T1] PCAOB Data Point 2025, findings; (4) *Stability tests* — recompute with preferred vs. fallback tag aliases and require rankings not to flip; any metric whose peer percentile moves >1 decile under alias substitution is not benchmarkable for that firm.

## 5. Coverage and Quality of Specific Public Proxies

- **Gross-margin decomposition** — fully derivable. COGS, GrossProfit, R&D, SG&A, SBC, and lease lines exist in `us-gaap` and Damodaran's margin table; contribution-margin proxies (gross margin minus marketing minus variable fulfillment) are computable for filers that break out marketing within SG&A — roughly 1/2 to 2/3 of observed DTC/CPG cohorts. [T3] Putra, "Public DTC unit economics 2026," Methodology section, https://putraco.com/blog/public-dtc-unit-economics-2026-cac-gm-contribution/
- **Revenue per employee** — derivable with a narrative step: revenue from XBRL, headcount from Item 1 Human Capital (Reg S-K Item 101(c) employee-count disclosure), retrieved via full-text search and parsed. Published cross-stock medians cluster around $416K–$556K (n=146 tracked tech companies; n=1,036 US equities >$2B cap / >500 employees), with a rule of thumb near $400K–$450K at IPO and top-decile >$2.27M; the denominator is year-end headcount mixing FT/PT and retail vs. corporate definitions, so a 10-K pool is internally stable but not comparable to contractor-excluding private-company calculations. [T3] Mostly Metrics, "Revenue Per Employee: Top vs Bottom Public Tech," https://www.mostlymetrics.com/p/revenue-per-employee-analyzing-the-top-and-bottom-players ; FFFinstill, "15 Highest Revenue-Per-Employee Stocks," Methodology, https://fffinstill.com/blog/15-highest-revenue-per-employee-stocks-hyper-productive-businesses ; Covirage, "Revenue per employee: formula, benchmarks, examples," https://covirage.ai/blog/revenue-per-employee
- **Sales & marketing as % revenue** — derivable where firms tag/disclose `SellingAndMarketingExpense` or a marketing sub-line in MD&A; otherwise S&M is buried in untagged SG&A (observed: WRBY 15.8%, HIMS 39.6%, ELF 26.4% disclosed; YETI, OLPX embedded). [T3] Putra, ibid., Disclosure-quality section.
- **CAC/LTV surrogates** — only surrogates. The numerator (S&M spend) is public; the denominator (new customers/cohorts, churn, retention) is almost never in XBRL and is increasingly *withdrawn* from narrative (Netflix ended quarterly paid-membership disclosure in Q1 2025; last audited figure 301.6M members [T3] Putra & Co, "Netflix subscriber ceiling," https://eightx.co/blog/netflix-subscription-ceiling-arpu-dtc ). Only a minority of public companies disclose LTV:CAC; blended CAC is known to understate first-time CAC by 30–50%. [T3] Putra, "Public DTC unit economics 2026," Gap section.
- **Inventory turns, DSO/DPO/DIO** — the most dependable middle-layer set. All required elements are standard `us-gaap` tags. Published results: S&P-1500 aggregate CCC ~32 days; small-company medians DSO 42.7d, DPO 70.8d, DIO 108.4d, CCC 91.2d with wide industry spreads (software CCC −6.6d to −40.5d; apparel +130.5d; pharma +171.4d); the J.P. Morgan quarterly index is a longitudinal anchor. [T3] CalcMastery, "Cash Conversion Cycle Benchmarks by Industry (2026)," Tables; Ataski, "Small Company Financial Benchmarks," Tables; [T2] J.P. Morgan, "Working Capital Index 2024," Exhibits.

## 6. What Remains Unbenchmarkable From Public Sources

1. **True unit economics.** CAC, LTV, payback, churn, cohort retention, ARPU per cohort, and per-unit variable costs require customer- and order-level data that are voluntary, non-standard, and frequently non-audited (KPIs are disclosed outside financial statements and often carry warnings that "certain of our key performance indicators are subject to inherent challenges in measurement" [T3] Eightx, "BARK teardown," Limitations para, https://eightx.co/blog/us-teardown-bark ), and removable at management discretion — sometimes while the trend deteriorates. Only survey panels (Benchmarkit ~1,000 firms, High Alpha 800+) approximate this layer, and they are self-selected.

2. **Capability maturity.** CMMI/ISO maturity levels require on-site appraisals; the published corpus is appraisal-based and self-reported (2020–2025: 24,246 appraisals, 92% self-stated goal achievement, no third-party audit of outcomes), case studies, and model-derived estimates (the classic "Level 1→2 saves ~75% cost and errors" figures were *inferred* by Bayesian mapping of SEI level distributions onto a productivity-index database, not observed). [T2] CMMI Institute, "CMMI Technical Report — Performance Results 2026," Summary; [T1] Rifkin, "Climbing the SEI maturity model makes a difference," Methodology section, para 2; the literature concedes "the shortage of benchmarks or empirical data on SPI implementation." [T1] Hassan, et al., "A benchmarking program to support software process improvement adaptation," PMC9137942, Background section.

3. **Process-internal metrics.** Operational process throughput, defect/rework rates, lead times, and delivery stability are observable at all only via DORA-style surveys (self-reported, IT/software-biased) or dim proxies (restatements ~3%/yr for error rates; churn via periodic subscriber disclosures). They cannot be reconstructed from filings.

4. **Transformation yields.** The return on converting capability investment → output (capability-portfolio yield, initiative-level ROI, asset-layer capacity utilisation) is a private, managerial quantity with no mandated disclosure channel; only its downstream traces appear as margin deltas, and attributing those deltas to specific capability transformations is underdetermined from public data.

The practical conclusion for a strategy advisor: build capability/process claims on *derived indices* (margin/employee/working-capital decompositions and their peer percentile bands), treat unit-economics numbers as survey-layer estimates with stated uncertainty, and never assert capability-maturity or transformation yields without first-party data.
