# Agentic Harnesses and AI Strategy Advisors

This survey examines the design of agentic harnesses that could operate on a model of a business — a connected graph of assets, liabilities, and transformation processes — to optimise it. It covers tool-use agent architectures, the Model Context Protocol (MCP) tool ecosystem, planner–executor loops, memory and state, human-in-the-loop approval, and safe action-taking under constraints, and then surveys the emerging "AI CFO / AI strategist / autonomous operations" product and research landscape. It ends with design requirements for an agent that optimises nodes of a business graph without violating cashflow, legal, mission, values, or brand constraints.

## From Chains to Agents: Tool-Use Architectures and Planner–Executor Loops

The minimum viable harness is a "reason–act" loop. ReAct-style agents interleave reasoning with tool invocation, completing a full inference cycle per acting step rather than emitting plans wholesale [T1] Li 2025, COLING, §4.2, ¶3. Surveys that unify the field converge on a four-component cycle — profile, memory, planning, action — in which memory shapes planning, planning shapes action, and action updates memory [T1] From Language to Action, arXiv 2508.17281, §3, ¶2. A widely-reproduced distinction separates *workflows* (LLMs orchestrated along pre-defined code paths) from *agents* (LLMs that "dynamically direct their own processes and tool usage"); agents suit open-ended problems where the step count is unpredictable and "you must have some level of trust in its decision-making" [T2] Schluntz & Zhang 2024, Anthropic "Building Effective Agents", Workflows vs. Agents section, ¶1. The corollary, widely quoted, is that autonomy "makes them ideal for scaling tasks in trusted environments" — which puts the burden on the harness to make the environment trustworthy [T3] Simon Willison, 2024-12-20, https://simonwillison.net/2024/Dec/20/building-effective-agents/.

Two workflow patterns recur in candidate business harnesses. The **orchestrator-workers** pattern has a central LLM decompose a task into subtasks, delegate them to worker LLMs, and synthesise their output; it fits precisely when subtasks cannot be enumerated in advance [T2] Schluntz & Zhang 2024, Anthropic "Building Effective Agents", Orchestrator-workers section, ¶1. The **evaluator-optimizer** loop pairs a generating model with an evaluating model that critiques output until criteria are met; its fit condition — the LLM must be able to supply useful critique — implies a *measurable objective* must exist to close the loop [T2] Schluntz & Zhang 2024, Anthropic "Building Effective Agents", Evaluator-optimizer section, ¶1. Anthropic's guidance doubles as a warning: "the most successful implementations use simple, composable patterns rather than complex frameworks", so complexity should only be added when it "demonstrably improves outcomes" [T2] Schluntz & Zhang 2024, Anthropic "Building Effective Agents", Introduction section, ¶1.

Planning itself is the weakest link for long horizons. Surveys of LLM planning note that zero-shot chain-of-thought "often fail[s] to produce long-horizon plans", and that greedily-generated static plans become unexecutable in stochastic environments when an early action makes a later one invalid [T1] Li 2025, COLING, §4.3, ¶3. Tree- and graph-search methods (Tree-of-Thoughts, Graph-of-Thoughts, A*-based ToolChain) generate multiple trajectories and prune before committing, trading inference cost for robustness in exactly the way a business planner wants for irreversible decisions [T1] LLM-Based Agents for Tool Learning: A Survey, Springer 2025, §2.2, ¶4. Meta's practical advance, "fast and slow" experiments, shows that sequential Bayesian optimisation over a large action space collapses from weeks to hours by running short biased experiments in parallel with one long-run experiment — a template for an optimisation agent that needs learning loops on product, pricing, or spend decisions [T1] Feng, Daulton, Letham, Balandat & Bakshy 2025, §1, ¶1.

## The Tool Ecosystem Layer: MCP and Agent-Centric Environments

For a business-graph agent, tools connect the reasoning core to the ledger, ERP, commerce, and marketing systems. MCP is the de facto integration substrate: a JSON-RPC 2.0 protocol in which hosts connect over stateful sessions to servers exposing *resources* (data), *prompts* (templates), and *tools* (executable functions), with capability negotiation at connect time and server-to-client *sampling* enabling server-side agentic loops [T2] MCP Specification 2025-11-25, Basic section, lines 12–34. The architecture deliberately mirrors the Language Server Protocol's design — focused, isolated servers that combine seamlessly — a boundary discipline that maps neatly onto a business decomposition where accounting, inventory, CRM, and pricing live as isolated, individually-auditable servers [T2] MCP Specification 2024-11-05, Architecture section, §Design Principles, ¶1.

The protocol's security posture is explicit and must be carried into any business harness: "Tools represent arbitrary code execution and must be treated with appropriate caution"; hosts "must obtain explicit user consent before invoking any tool"; and because MCP itself cannot enforce security, implementors should build consent flows, access control, data protections, and audit [T2] MCP Specification 2025-11-25, Security and Trust section, ¶2–3. The November 2025 spec release added production-oriented machinery an optimisation agent needs: **tasks** (tracking long-running server work), **authorization extensions** for machine-to-machine OAuth and enterprise IdP policy, **URL-mode elicitation** so credentials never transit the client, and **sampling-with-tools** so servers can run their own agentic loops under the user's tokens [T2] MCP Core Maintainers 2025, "One Year of MCP" post, ¶5–13.

The ecosystem, however, is still maturing on safety. AgentBound — the first access-control framework for MCP servers — found "thousands of MCP servers execute with unrestricted access to host systems, creating a broad attack surface"; its Android-style permission manifests auto-generate from server source at 80.9% accuracy and its enforcement engine mitigates tool-poisoning, puppetry, and rug-pull attacks with ~0.6 ms overhead [T1] Bühler, Biagiola, Di Grazia & Salvaneschi 2025, §1, ¶1. Commercially this matters: PwC's May 2025 survey of 308 US executives found 79% already adopting agents but only ~19% connecting them across applications and workflows, and argues an agentic organisation needs an "operating system... to orchestrate multiple agents from multiple vendors across complex business processes" [T2] PwC 2025, AI Agent Survey, Key findings section, ¶3, ¶8.

## Memory and State over Business Timescales

A business is a slowly-evolving system with feedback delays (billing cycles, R&D, churn), so an optimising agent is only as good as its memory architecture. Recent survey work has moved from crude short/long-term dichotomies to a functional taxonomy of **factual** memory (what the agent has learned), **experiential** memory (skills accumulated across tasks), and **working** memory (active per-task state), in token-level, parametric, or latent forms [T1] Memory in the Age of AI Agents, arXiv 2512.13564, §2, ¶3. An operation-centric view formalises memory as a write–manage–read loop with six atomic operations — consolidation, indexing, updating, forgetting, retrieval, compression — the practical checklist for an agent that must forget stale forecasts and re-model the business graph [T1] Du et al. 2025, §1, ¶2; [T1] Zhang et al., ACM TOIS, §5, ¶4.

The empirical picture is sobering. **Vending-Bench** — a simulated vending-machine business where an agent orders stock, prices, and pays daily fees over 20M-token runs — shows that frontier models manage the machine well in some runs and produce profits above the human baseline, but *every* model "has runs that derail", typically by misreading delivery status and then descending into tangential "meltdown loops" from which they rarely recover [T1] Backlund & Petersson 2025, §1, ¶1 and §4, ¶3. In the most cited failure, a Sonnet run decides to "close" the business then attempts to contact the FBI when the $2 daily fee keeps being charged [T3] Andon Labs, https://andonlabs.com/evals/vending-bench. Two findings transfer directly to a business-graph harness: failures show **no correlation with context-window exhaustion** (so more context is not the fix), and behavioural variance across runs is high, meaning determinism must be supplied by the harness rather than the model [T1] Backlund & Petersson 2025, §4, ¶1–2.

**CEO-Bench** extends this to a fuller setting: an agent runs a simulated startup for 500 days through a programmable Python interface with 34 tools and a 19-table database, facing noisy, partially-observable state, delayed effects, and non-stationary markets. Only the strongest models cross the $1M starting balance and "all evaluated models remain below the rule-based baseline", yet the best agents write sophisticated forecasting code for churn, billing, and cash [T1] CEO-Bench, arXiv 2606.18543, §1, ¶1, §6, ¶2.

## Safe Action-Taking: Guardrails, Approval Loops, Constraint Enforcement

Because an optimiser's actions mutate a business, safety research is directly applicable. Guardrail agents sit outside the decision-model and deterministically check each proposed action. **GuardAgent** translates human-written safety requests into a task plan, maps it to guardrail code, and executes it, achieving >98% guardrail accuracy on a healthcare access-control benchmark and 83% on a web-safety benchmark [T1] Xiang et al. 2025, ICML 2025, §1, ¶1. **ShieldAgent** extracts verifiable rules from policy documents into "action-based probabilistic rule circuits" and shields a target agent's whole trajectory, outperforming prior methods by 11.3% on average with 90.1% recall while cutting API queries 64.7% — evidence that policy-checking is cheaper than more model calls [T1] Chen, Kang & Li 2025, ICML 2025, §1, ¶1.

A crucial design choice emerging in 2025 is *least privilege with monotonic confinement*. **Progent** represents privilege as symbolic rules over tool names and arguments, checks every tool call deterministically, and lets the LLM propose policy updates; an SMT solver classifies each update as a *narrowing* (applied automatically) or an *expansion* (requires human approval), so the agent's effective action space "can only narrow without approval — silent privilege escalation is impossible even under adversarial input". In practice only ~6% of updates needed approval, so the human cost of strict confinement is low [T1] Progent, arXiv 2504.11703, §1, ¶1, §3, ¶1. This is exactly the structure a business-graph optimiser needs: a policy layer that *can* grant a new tool (raise a price, change a term, spend budget) but only via an auditable, human-authorized expansion. A parallel line specifies constraints as a lightweight DSL (triggers, predicates, enforcements such as `user_inspection` or `llm_self_examine`), preventing ~90%+ of unsafe executions in code agents [T1] "\tool" framework, arXiv 2503.18666, §1, ¶1. At the MCP boundary itself, schema validation, formal pre-/post-conditions, rate limits, and loop-prevention are proposed as the enterprise enforcement layer [T1] Rohatgi 2025, IJIRMPS, §5, ¶1.

Two further points matter for deployments. Feedback divides into internal, external, multi-agent, and human channels, and the human channel encodes mission, values, and brand judgements no ledger contains [T1] Xiang et al., IJCAI 2025, §2.2, ¶1. Anthropic's "Project Vend" catalogues the failures prompting approval gates: selling items below cost, hallucinating a payment account, getting talked into discount codes, and not learning from mistakes. The authors attribute most failures to scaffolding, concluding that "AI middle-managers are plausibly on the horizon" once tools, memory, CRM, and structured reflection improve [T2] Anthropic, Project Vend (2025), Outcomes and analysis section, ¶3–6.

## The AI CFO and Autonomous Finance Layer

A wave of commercial agents already reads live ledger data and proposes (rarely executes) changes; together they define the functional spec for a finance-aware node-optimiser. The recurring architecture is **read-only, source-cited, draft-and-approve**:

- **FCFO.ai's AI CFO** works only from sanitised, structured data, attaches source references and confidence flags to every figure, routes every material output through a human-approved draft, routes all model calls through a gateway that rejects raw documents/credentials, and logs every request to a tamper-evident trail [T2] FCFO.ai product page, "AI CFO" section, ¶2, ¶5.
- **Auditoria SmartResearch** couples ERP/EPM integration (Workday, Oracle, SAP, Microsoft) with external intelligence feeds (S&P Capital IQ, Moody's, Refinitiv, FRED, BLS) and markets "explainable outputs with traceability" on every recommendation [T2] Auditoria.AI press release, 2025-09-09, ¶2–3.
- **Prowl Peregrine CFO** (QuickBooks Online) is explicit about scope: "100% AI actions logged & human-approved", risky changes become *Proposed Actions* with risk level, role gate, and expiration; the model operates only within validated entity scope and cannot switch tenants, and tool access is role-gated [T2] Prowl Systems, Peregrine CFO product page, Features and "Grounded, never hallucinated" sections, ¶1–4.
- **Zeni's AI CFO** runs scenario planning ("cut vendor spend by 12%… see the new runway") on real-time books maintained by a separate AI Accountant agent, with no write-back from the strategy agent [T2] Zeni, AI CFO Agent page, Workflow section, ¶1–6.
- **Nume** positions itself as proactive and read-only: it monitors books, banks, payments, payroll, "proactively reaches out", drafts next steps, and cannot "modify, delete or transfer your data" [T2] Nume.ai, Product page, ¶2–4.
- **Prophix One Agents** go further toward execution (budgeting, reporting, modelling) and claim explicit guardrails: "policy-first guardrails, full audit trails and explainable outputs", plus a 50% cut in monthly close time from internal use — early evidence of measurable finance automation [T2] Prophix press release, 2025-09-23, ¶3, ¶6.

On the research side, **FinAgent** demonstrates a fully autonomous financial-decision architecture (market intelligence → dual-level reflection → diversified memory retrieval → tool-augmented decisions), beating 12 baselines by >36% average profit across six datasets while always emitting reasoning for its buy/sell/hold actions — evidence that reflection-plus-memory is a working optimizer loop [T1] Zhang et al., KDD 2024, §4, ¶1, §8, ¶1.

For an optimiser acting on a business graph, the consistent commercial lesson is a *reversibility ladder*: pure reads (Q&A, variance analysis) run autonomously; state changes (journal entries, budget edits) are drafted and human-approved; nothing touches the books unsupervised.

## Autonomous Experimentation and Strategy Agents

The closest thing to a general business optimizer is the autonomous experiment layer. Established experimentation platforms supply the causal machinery — sticky assignment, holdouts, sequential testing with mSPRT, multi-armed bandits, and automated safety rollback on error-rate/latency thresholds [T2] Experimently, product page, ¶1–2. Meta's "fast and slow" work shows the statistical core can be automated end-to-end: a BO loop around pilot A/B tests achieved >60% faster wall-clock optimization while saving >60% of experiment resources, and at sub-daily trial cadence the loop runs "fully-automated, requiring no human intervention" [T1] Feng et al. 2025, §3.1–3.2, ¶2.

Newer "agent-first" products fuse the experiment layer with an LLM controller over MCP. **Levered** lets an agent define a reward goal, generates variants, wires tracking, and continuously shifts traffic to winners with contextual/RL algorithms, holdouts for incrementality, and no UI-only path — "optimization at agent speed" [T2] Levered, product page, ¶1–5. Comparable bandit services expose MCP tools (`optimize_bandit`, `optimize_contextual`) so agents can run decisions on pure, interpretable math rather than more LLM calls [T3] @oraclaw/bandit, npm package, §Features, ¶2. A governance-rich open-source reference shows the production scaffolding such systems need: offline policy evaluation (IPS/SNIPS/doubly-robust), governance statuses (Continue / Hold / Human Review / Rollback), traffic caps, canary percentages, and constrained generation from approved templates that "always requir[es] human review" [T3] davidwrauch, adaptive-experimentation-platform, GitHub README, §Governance, ¶1–3.

Strategy-level simulation is maturing into a benchmarking discipline. **Back Bay Battery** (BBB) — an eight-period R&D allocation game with a cash cow (~80% revenue), an emerging technology, and uncertain feedback — was used to benchmark 21 proprietary and 13 open-source LLMs against MBA cohorts. Reasoning-focused late-2024 models (o4-mini, Claude Sonnet 4, Gemini 2.0 Flash) "exceeded even the average scores of historical MBA cohorts", yet mid-2025 frontier models *declined*, underperforming earlier LLMs and MBA students, with "a systematic bias toward exploiting the core business at the expense of investing in future growth" [T1] Allen & McDonald, Strategy Science 2026, Abstract, ¶1–2. The same exploitation-bias appears in a separate month-by-month retail simulator where five free-tier LLMs made pricing, hiring, marketing, and loan decisions; the authors' method — feeding a structured full-business report each period and scoring profit, revenue, and market share — is itself a reusable harness design [T1] AI Playing Business Games, arXiv 2509.26331, Abstract, ¶1.

For human-AI collaboration on strategy, **StrategicAI** decomposes strategic questions into logic trees that humans and retrievers jointly expand, with the AI a multi-agent RAG system — "users are in full control of the tree decompositions" [T1] StrategicAI, ECML PKDD 2025, Abstract, ¶1. BCG's Henderson Institute uses LLM-powered multi-agent simulation (customers, regulators, competitors as role-played agents) for war-gaming, reproducing three of a client's workshop recommendations and surfacing two novel options; Columbia research cited therein shows multi-agent simulation improves accuracy of human-behaviour modelling by ~75% over single-agent [T3] Candelon et al., Fortune 2025-07-11, ¶4–7. Strategic Management Journal research shows LLM agents can serve as "model organisms" for strategy — replicating patterns from human experiments on exploration-exploitation spillovers, with value "less in discovering new mechanisms than in generating hypotheses that merit empirical validation" [T1] Tranchero et al., SMJ 2026, Conclusion section, ¶1–2.

Finally, the physical-world pilot **Project Vend** defines the frontier of *execution*: an agent that actually ran inventory, pricing, and customer relations. Its failures (below-cost pricing, hallucinated payment account, discount-code manipulation) are a direct catalogue of what a brand/values-aware harness must police, and its diagnosis — that stronger scaffolding, not just stronger models, is the fix — is the current consensus [T2] Anthropic, Project Vend, Outcomes and analysis section, ¶3–6.

## Design Requirements for a Constraint-Safe Business-Graph Optimiser

Synthesising the above, an agent intended to optimise nodes of a business graph — without violating cashflow, legal, mission, values, and brand constraints — should meet these requirements:

1. **Double-loop controller.** A planner–executor loop for episode-level actions (evaluator-optimizer where a measurable P&L proxy exists) wrapped in an orchestrator–workers layer that decomposes a goal into per-node experiments [T2] Schluntz & Zhang 2024, Anthropic "Building Effective Agents", Orchestrator-workers section, ¶1; [T1] Li 2025, COLING, §4.2, ¶3.
2. **MCP-native, capability-negotiated tool surface.** Every read and write goes through servers exposing resources (ledger, inventory, contracts) and tools (reprice, reorder, budget, message), with capability negotiation and consent on every call per MCP security guidance [T2] MCP Specification 2025-11-25, Security and Trust section, ¶2–3; and a least-privilege enforcement layer (AgentBound-style manifests) outside the reasoning model [T1] Bühler et al. 2025, §1, ¶1.
3. **Monotonic-confinement permissioning.** The policy layer can only shrink automatically; every expansion of the action space requires explicit human approval, SMT-verified so no injected instruction can silently escalate privilege [T1] Progent, arXiv 2504.11703, §1, ¶1. Practitioners add the operational envelope: hard and soft limits per tool, emergency brakes visible to the agent, gateways to approved ordering software rather than open email, and review dashboards with human natural-language feedback [T3] Matt Webb, 2025-07-10, https://interconnected.org/home/2025/07/10/vending.
4. **Reversibility ladder.** Reads autonomous, writes drafted-and-approved, external commitments (contracts, discount codes, payment instructions) gated behind role-based approval with a full audit trail — the pattern common to Peregrine, FCFO, Zeni, and Nume [T2] Prowl Systems, Peregrine CFO, Features section, ¶3; [T2] FCFO.ai, AI CFO page, ¶5.
5. **Explicit long-horizon state management.** A write–manage–read memory system (consolidation, indexing, updating, forgetting, retrieval) rather than raw context growth, because Vending-Bench and CEO-Bench show breakdowns are not context-bound [T1] Du et al. 2025, §1, ¶2; [T1] Backlund & Petersson 2025, §4, ¶2. Factual/experiential/working memory should be separated, matching an object store for the business graph, an experiment log, and per-session working state [T1] Memory in the Age of AI Agents, arXiv 2512.13564, §2, ¶3.
6. **Falsification over optimisation.** Where causal evidence is obtainable (pricing, spend, messaging), hand off to holdouts, sequential stopping, and bandit/BO engines rather than letting the LLM close the statistical loop outright [T1] Feng et al. 2025, §1, ¶1; [T3] davidwrauch, adaptive-experimentation-platform, README, §Governance, ¶2.
7. **Constraint encoding separate from intent.** Cashflow, legal, mission, values, and brand rules should be compiled into verifiable guardrails (GuardAgent/ShieldAgent circuits, MCP schema contracts) checked deterministically before execution, not merely prompted [T1] Chen, Kang & Li 2025, ICML 2025, §1, ¶1; [T1] Rohatgi 2025, IJIRMPS, §5, ¶1.
8. **Executive-summary acting.** Values and brand judgements that resist formalisation enter through the human approval channel; the harness should surface uncertainty, cite every number's source, and mark material output as drafts for accountable humans [T1] Xiang et al., IJCAI 2025, §2.2, ¶1; [T2] FCFO.ai, AI CFO page, ¶2.
9. **Sandboxed rehearsal first.** Replay the controller against a deterministic simulation of the business before touching live state [T2] Anthropic, Building Effective Agents, §Agents section, ¶1; [T1] AI Playing Business Games, arXiv 2509.26331, Abstract, ¶1.
10. **Evaluation harness, not just agent.** A business-graph agent is an agent *plus* a harness; grade it in an isolated, reproducible eval environment with deterministic graders where possible [T2] Anthropic, Demystifying Evals for AI Agents (2026), ¶3–4 — with BBB/CEO-Bench-style composite scores (profit, growth, future position) rather than a single metric [T1] Allen & McDonald, Strategy Science 2026, §2, ¶2.

The empirical record is unambiguous about where the risk lives: not in tool access, which permissioning solves, but in long-horizon coherence and in optimising the wrong objective (exploitation bias). A harness that supplies determinism, memory discipline, monotonic confinement, and a human approval channel to a strong model is the disciplined construction — and products and benchmarks agree the gap between "plausible actions" and "sustained, constraint-respecting growth" is closing from the harness side.
