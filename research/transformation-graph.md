# The Business as a Transformation Graph

A business can be modelled as a directed, heterogeneous graph: nodes are the things the firm owns, owes, makes, sells, and interacts with (assets, liabilities, inventories, capabilities, actors, contracts, products, customers, channels); edges are the flows and transformations that move matter, value, cost, and cash between them over time. A P&L and balance sheet are then *aggregate projections* of that graph — the coupled state and flows at the firm boundary — and the purpose of the graph is to let a human or an agentic advisor reason about, simulate, and optimise the underlying machine without breaking those projections. This file assembles those traditions — REA resource-flow networks, enterprise knowledge graphs and digital twins, process-event graphs, capability and business-model ontologies, and driver-based decomposition — into a working catalog of node types, edge types, and quantitative annotations.

## From financial statements to an execution fabric

The financial report is itself a knowledge graph. A balance sheet represents state ("stocks"); the income, equity, and cash-flow statements represent changes in state ("flows"), connected by aggregation rules [T2] Hoffman, *Financial Reports are Knowledge Graphs* (xbrlsite.com). The four statements are *articulated* — mathematically interrelated by the double-entry rule (debits = credits) and the accounting equation (assets = liabilities + equity), so net income flows into retained earnings [T2] Hoffman & van Egmond, *Financial Report Semantics and Dynamics Theory*, §3.2. Any node-and-edge model of the business must therefore project to these statements without contradiction.

The strategy and system-dynamics literatures describe the object in the same relational terms. A business model is "an architecture for how a firm creates and delivers value to customers and the mechanisms employed to capture a share of that value" [T1] Teece, *Long Range Planning* 51(1), §1; business models are "a distinct and holistic configuration of interrelated components" whose linkages "impose feedback and circular causality" [T1] Moellers et al. 2019, *Electronic Markets*, §1. System dynamics supplies the stock-and-flow primitives: "Stocks include inventories of products, and financial accounts, such as debt, book value, and cash. Flows are the rates of increase or decrease in stocks, such as production and shipment, borrowing and repayment, investment and depreciation" [T1] Khaledi 2015, §1.

Real process data is intrinsically graph-shaped. Conventional process mining forces events onto a single "case," producing deficiency (dropped events), convergence (duplicated events), and divergence (wrong precedence) [T1] van der Aalst 2019, in Berti et al. 2023, §1. Object-centric event data (OCED) treats an event graph of interacting objects as primary; flattening it to sequences loses "an average 41.7% performance increase across all predictive tasks," recovered by graph encodings and graph embeddings [T1] Adams, Park & van der Aalst, §7. Digital-twin-of-organization (DTO) practice institutionalises the same move: Gartner defines a DTO as "a dynamic software model that relies on operational and contextual data to understand how an organization operationalizes its business model, …" with platform capabilities modelled as processes/tasks, resources (machines, IT systems, people, agents, time, finance), deliverables, and client/supplier/stakeholder interactions [T2] Gartner, *Market Guide for DTO Platforms*, 20 Nov 2024, "What are DTO platforms" ¶1.

The intellectual ancestor is REA (Resources, Events, Agents), McCarthy's 1982 reformulation of accounting as a graph rather than a ledger: goods, services or money (resources), the transactions or agreements affecting them (events), and the people or agencies performing them (agents) [T3] Wikipedia, §1. At the top of REA's architecture sits the *enterprise value chain*: "a chain of economic exchanges … each time giving up an economic resource (perhaps money) in return for another resource of greater value," value being the deliverable portfolio of product or service attributes attractive to the ultimate customers [T1] Geerts & McCarthy 1999, in Dunn, Gerard & Grabski 2016, §2 ¶7.

## Node types: a convergent catalog

Across REA, ArchiMate, e³value, the Business Model Ontology (BMO), DEMO, and DTO practice, the node taxonomies are strikingly convergent; the union below forms the working catalog.

**Assets, liabilities, inventories (Economic Resources).** REA's core node, the economic resource, is something scarce, with utility, under enterprise control; one instance plays many roles over its life — "a machine is first acquired (take), then employed in production (consumed), and finally sold (give)" [T1] Geerts & McCarthy 2000, §2. Inventory/WIP and fixed assets are the same node type in different transformation states, which is why REA refuses the ledger's arbitrary separation of asset classes [T1] Dunn, Gerard & Grabski 2016, §1 ¶1.

**Capabilities.** ArchiMate 3.1 defines a **capability** as "an ability that an active structure element, such as an organization, person, or system, possesses," and a **resource** as "an asset owned or controlled by an individual or organization" [T2] The Open Group, *ArchiMate 3.1 Specification*, Strategy Layer. Capabilities are the firm's "potential"; value streams are that potential "in action," one "single, reusable unit of potential" serving several value streams at once [T1] COVO, CEUR WS Vol-4171, §1. The resource-based view adds the decision-relevant property: VRIN resources are the seat of durable advantage only when "harnessed to a coherent strategy and a sound business model" [T1] Teece 2018, §2.

**Actors and roles.** REA's **agents** divide into inside (employee) and outside (customer, supplier) roles, with *accountability* as a subtype of the inside participation relation [T1] Geerts & McCarthy 2000, §2. DEMO refines this with *transactor roles*: "an actor role is defined as the authority and responsibility to be the executor of a transaction type," which do not map one-to-one to departments or the chart of accounts [T1] Dietz 2006, §2.

**Contracts and commitments.** REA layers a *commitment level* (planned/scheduled) and a *policy level* (could/should/must happen) over the operational level (what happened) [T1] Dunn, Gerard & Grabski 2016, §1 ¶6. Commitments are first-class graph elements: a claim against an outside agent exists exactly when a resource flows without "the full set of corresponding instances of a dual flow" — a condition a graph engine can derive declaratively [T1] Geerts & McCarthy 2000a, p.136, in Dunn et al. 2016, §2 ¶3. DEMO makes commitment the enterprise's backbone: a business process is "a tree of enclosed transactions" and "an organization is a network of actors and transactions" [T1] Dietz 2006, §2–§3. Contracts are thus *obligation-carrying edge bundles* between actor nodes and resource or value-stream nodes.

**Products, customers, channels.** ArchiMate's **Product** is "a coherent collection of services and/or passive structure elements, accompanied by a set of agreements, which is offered as a whole to (internal or external) customers" [T2] The Open Group, *ArchiMate 3.1 Specification*, Business Layer. The BMO contributes value proposition, customer segment, distribution channel, and relationship; e³value contributes customer needs as quantified stimuli that start value exchanges [T1] Gordijn, Osterwalder & Pigneur 2005, §3.3. Customers and channels are boundary nodes, DTO practice extending the model outward with "an increasing degree of information from customers, partners and public sources" [T1] Riss et al. 2020, §3.

Node-typing rules come from DTO practice — process/task, resource, deliverable, and stakeholder as separate node classes [T2] Gartner, *Market Guide for DTO Platforms*, "Business Operations Model Capabilities" ¶1 — and from COVO: one primary capability per value-stream stage, one business object per capability at each granularity level, so action stays traceable to accountable nodes [T1] COVO, CEUR WS Vol-4171, §C6, §C9.

## Edge types: how value, cost, and cash move

REA's stock-flow taxonomy is the canonical edge catalog. The five relations — **use, consume, give, take, produce** — connect economic resources to economic events, which pair by *duality* into *transfers* (value created in a market transaction) and *transformations* (value created by change in form or substance) [T1] Geerts & McCarthy 2000, §2. Consumption decrements resources "in chunks that leave the original form discernible," whereas use "completely disappears" them [T1] ibid. So **flows** are give/take and **transformations** produce/use/consume.

**Ownership and control.** The take relation creates ownership edges; REA separates custody, association, and linkage relations from legal ownership, so control is separable from title [T1] Geerts & McCarthy 2000, §Conclusion.

**Obligations.** Commitments and the time-delayed dual ("claim materialization") turn obligations into edges that exist *because a flow lacks its mirror* — the graph's consistency engine generates them [T1] Geerts & McCarthy 2000a in Dunn et al. 2016, §2 ¶3.

**Influence and enablement.** ArchiMate's dynamic family (flow, triggering) and strategy-layer *influence* relation cover material/information flow and enablement [T2] Sparx Systems, *ArchiMate Modeling Language 17.2 User Guide*, §Relationships. e³value makes influence quantitative: value exchanges carry valuation functions and needs carry occurrence counts, from which a tool "generates a profitability sheet showing ingoing and outgoing cash flows" [T1] Gordijn, Osterwalder & Pigneur 2005, §3.3. This is the optimisation crux: value, cost, and cash move along *different edge types at different rates*, and the graph must hold all three — value along valuation edges, cost along activity/driver edges, cash along settlement edges — with payment events lagging revenue events exactly as the claim rule predicts.

**Cost edges.** Activity-based costing distinguishes "the cost of resources used and the cost of resources supplied," whose difference is "the cost of resources not used" [T2] Porto FEP WP 199, *Aggregation in activity-based costing*, §2 — idling capacity accrues a charge on unused edge capacity, invisible in a pure flow model. And "the fundamental ABC property of linearity between costs and output does not generally hold in the short run," so costs are not simply linear edge weights [T2] ibid., §1. Driver-based planning is the practical compromise: a small driver set (volume, rates, conversion ratios, costs, churn) calibrated against financial outcomes, "prioritiz[ing] speed and simplicity" [T2] AFP, *FP&A Guide to Driver-based Models and Plans*, §What is a driver-based model.

## Time and quantities on edges: yields, throughput, cycle time, capacity

Lean value-stream mapping (VSM) supplies the meters: **cycle time** (time per unit), **lead time** (end-to-end time), **uptime**, **changeover time**, **% complete & accurate** (first-pass yield), and **availability** [T2] Lean Enterprise Institute, *Value Stream Mapping* lexicon, §Data boxes; [T2] MIT OCW 16.660, §VSM. **Takt time** = available time ÷ customer demand anchors the graph to the market [T2] MIT OCW, §Takt and cycle times. The telling insight: lead time is dominated by *waiting between* transformations — an example stream with ~7.8 minutes of touch time carries ~8 demand-days of WIP across four queues, cut to ~1.6 demand-days by batching, scheduling, and WIP-control changes alone, not speed [T3] LearnLeanSigma, §Future-state. WIP is a stock the graph can value; LEI reads lead time off inventory ÷ demand [T2] LEI, ibid.

Theory of Constraints (TOC) supplies the capacity discipline: throughput is set by the constraint — "total process throughput can only be improved when the constraint is improved" [T3] leanproduction.com, §Core concept. Throughput accounting redefines the three cardinal stocks: **Throughput T** = sales revenue − totally variable cost; **Investment I** = money tied up in the system; **Operating Expense OE** = money spent converting inputs to output, with Net Profit = T − OE [T2] Budd, *Theory of Constraints Handbook* ch.13, §TOC measures; [T3] Wikipedia, *Throughput accounting*, §Measures. T is generated only at sale — producing for the warehouse is investment, not throughput [T3] ibid. Choosing product mix by throughput-per-bottleneck-hour raised one metal shop's profitability by two-thirds [T3] Wikipedia, ibid., §Constraint-based decisions.

The constraint generalises to a graph rule: whichever edge or node is the constraint — internal resource, market, channel, or cash — sets where a unit of improvement has system-level effect, and TOC's five focusing steps (identify → exploit → subordinate → elevate → repeat) are a fixed-point iteration on the graph [T3] leanproduction.com, §The Five Focusing Steps. GAAP inventory valuation can likewise "punish a required FG stock reduction," a warning for any graph whose edges claim P&L authority [T3] dralanbarnard.com, §Mistake 1.

Object-centric process mining automates the metering: the object-centric directly-follows multigraph (OC-DFG) is annotated with frequencies and, per edge, average durations and performance measures — e.g. "the average time between placing an order and the last item being delivered," unanswerable in single-case mining [T1] van der Aalst & Berti 2022, *OC-PM*, §1 & Def. 16. Extraction from ERP systems is itself graph-guided — a "Graph of Relationships" over tables identifies process-relevant tables (order-to-cash, procure-to-pay) [T1] Berti et al. 2021, §2; ontology-based data access formalises mapping legacy schemas to event logs [T1] Calvanese et al. 2023, §3.

## Reconciliation with the financial statements

The statements are the graph's control layer. XBRL-based reporting is already formalised as a *logical system*: the double-entry rule, the accounting equation, a core statement-element set, and **articulation** — "the four core statements … are all mathematically interrelated" [T2] Hoffman & van Egmond, *Financial Report Semantics and Dynamics Theory*, §3.2. The same logical theory serialises into RDF/OWL/SHACL, Cypher, Prolog, or XBRL — "be suspicious of software vendors that tell you that you must use one specific technical format" [T2] Hoffman, *Representing a Logic System Using XBRL*, §Knowledge graphs are format-independent. A graph model can therefore declare the four statements as *derived views with enforced invariants*, as REA intended reports to be derivable projections of shared event data, not parallel stores that "destroy details … that may facilitate decision making" [T1] Dunn, Gerard & Grabski 2016, §1 ¶1.

DuPont analysis is the canonical *ratio bridge* between the two halves, linking margin ("the strategic effectiveness of the organization") to asset turnover and leverage ("how hard we are making the assets work") [T3] Amrish Shah, *FP&A Trends*, §7; the five-step decomposition isolates tax burden, interest burden, operating margin, turnover, and leverage [T3] Investopedia, §Formula. The decomposition is predictive, not decorative: separating return on net operating assets into margin and turnover *improves out-of-sample prediction of future profitability*, holding up in updated data since the early 2000s [T1] Soliman 2008, cited in *Earnings prediction with DuPont components*, *Review of Accounting Studies* 2023, §1. For a graph it is a schema of multiplicative constraints — and an optimisation surface, since every factor is a lever.

Two cautions keep the reconciliation honest. Throughput accounting departs from GAAP deliberately for *decision* purposes (valuing inventory at raw-material cost only; splitting COGS into totally variable cost and operating expense) while "TA does not replace the need to prepare formal company accounts" [T3] Wikipedia, §1. Decision-metric and reporting-metric edges must be *separate, reconcilable projections*, not rivals for truth. Driver-based models need "an overall analytical framework of the company that defines the relevant metrics, defined taxonomy and approved data for usage," tested against benchmarks, sensitivity, and stress [T2] AFP, *FP&A Guide*, §Identify/quantify drivers. System-dynamics practice gives the protocol — "begin with the financial relations and standard accounting equations. Then we look for the decisions and operational variables that govern the financial variables" — reverse-engineering the P&L into its driver graph [T1] Khaledi 2015, §1.

## Schema, bitemporality, and granularity

**Schema.** The workhorse representation is the labeled property graph: vertices and directed edges with type labels and key/value properties, natively a multigraph [T1] Böhm et al. 2021, §2 ¶1. For the process/event domain, OCEL stores events, objects, and many-to-many event–object relations [T1] Farhang Ghahfarokhi, Park, Berti & van der Aalst 2020, §2. Its IEEE OCED successor is operationalised as *event knowledge graphs* combining "the structural dimension (objects, relationships, and their attributes) and the temporal dimension (how events create and change structure over time)" [T1] Swevels, Fahland & Montali 2024, §1–§3.

**Time and versioning.** Edges must be bitemporal: *valid time* (when a fact held in the modelled world) versus *transaction time* (when the database knew it), giving a snapshot per (valid, transaction) instant [T1] Chekol & Stuckenschmidt 2019, §Def. 1–2; [T1] Böhm et al. 2021, §1. Implementation spans storage ("anchor+delta" compression so any past version is reconstructable) [T1] Zhang et al. 2023, *AeonG*, §3–4 — and modelling patterns: time-stamped properties, versioned value nodes, temporal edges with `valid_from`/`valid_to` and `SUPERSEDED_BY` chains, and Type-2 slowly-changing-dimension handling for contract, credit, and price tiers; obligations matter doubly, since terms, prices, and policies change governance without changing identity [T2] McCreary, *Context Graph* ch.13, §Slowly changing dimension.

**Granularity.** REA's three levels — *enterprise script*, *process*, *task* — prescribe a depth hierarchy [T1] Geerts & McCarthy 2000, §3. DEMO counter-balances: abstracting away implementation (functions, departments, ICT) and keeping only the performative essence cuts reported modelling effort ~70% while staying valid across reorganisations — granularity is "deep enough to carry the decision," not "as deep as possible" [T1] Dietz 2006, §1–§2. COVO constrains coherence across levels: downward coherence requires high-level relationships be grounded in at least one child pair, and "non-hierarchical relationships may only occur between elements at the same granularity level" [T1] COVO, CEUR WS Vol-4171, §C4–C5.

## What an optimiser can operate on

Assembled, the transformation graph gives an agentic advisor four classes of handles. **Nodes**: stocks of cash, inventory, WIP, AR, capacity, and their commitments, valued in money (reporting) and in TOC terms (decision). **Edges**: yields (% complete & accurate), cycle and lead times, uptime, changeover, throughput rates, valuations, and driver coefficients — parameters an initiative can change. **Structure**: which transformations exist, are outsourced, or are bottlenecked — the make-or-buy question where "controlling bottleneck assets is critical to capturing value" [T1] Teece 2018, §5, and the constraint TOC says governs all improvement [T3] leanproduction.com, §Core concept. **Meta-edges**: the bitemporal commitment ledger and the dynamic capabilities of sensing–seizing–transforming that redesign the graph itself [T1] Teece, Pisano & Shuen 1997, §Conclusion. Because the statements articulate as hard invariants [T2] Hoffman & van Egmond, §3.2, every proposed mutation can be *pre-validated* against projected P&L, balance sheet, and cash flow before it touches reality — the graph is then not a diagram but a harness: simulate the change, check the projections, dispatch the experiment.
