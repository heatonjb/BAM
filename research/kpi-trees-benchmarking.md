# KPI Trees and Comparative Benchmarking at Each Layer

If a business is a transformation machine — a graph of assets, liabilities, and transformations that convert inputs into sellable outputs — then metrics are its observable state variables and benchmarks are the reference distributions against which those states are judged. A KPI tree attaches a measurable quantity to each node and edge; a benchmarking dataset supplies the population that tells an operator whether a node's value is good, bad, or typical. This document maps those families onto layers of the business graph, then surveys benchmark data sources, costs, licensing, peer-matching, and failure modes.

## Metric hierarchies and the layers they attach to

Metric frameworks differ along two axes: how deep they decompose, and which layer of the graph they observe. The practical families are North Star frameworks, algebraic KPI/metric trees, OKRs, the Balanced Scorecard, and financial decompositions such as DuPont and driver-based planning.

**North Star frameworks** place a single value-delivery metric at the apex and three to six input metrics beneath it. Three tests define it: it captures value *received* rather than extracted, leads rather than lags revenue, and is movable by teams — which disqualifies revenue and signups and qualifies Spotify's "time listening" or Airbnb's "nights booked" [T3] Prakash Poudel Sharma, https://www.sharmaprakash.com.np/product-management/north-star-metrics-and-metric-trees/. Practitioners distinguish an *algebraic* KPI tree, whose links are fixed mathematical identities, from a *probabilistic* North Star map, whose links are testable hypotheses [T2] Mixpanel, "North Star framework vs. KPI tree", §"Algebraic KPI trees".

**KPI/metric trees** extend this to whole-of-business scope: three to five levels, dozens of nodes, each with a named owner, and every parent-child edge a testable causal hypothesis [T3] KPI Tree, https://kpitree.co/guides/frameworks/north-star-framework-vs-metric-trees. The North Star becomes the root and decomposition continues until every leaf maps to one team [T3] KPI Tree, https://kpitree.co/guides/core-concepts/north-star-metric. This attaches metrics to the *operational* layer — the processes that perform transformations — and supplies a diagnostic path from symptom to root cause.

**OKRs** attach metrics to the *intention* layer: objectives express direction, key results are specific, time-bound, verifiable numbers, and the cadence is quarterly. The framework originated with Andy Grove at Intel in the 1970s (as "iMBOs", derived from Drucker's Management by Objectives), was carried to Google by John Doerr in 1999, and is deliberately decoupled from compensation [T2] WIRED, "When John Doerr Brought a 'Gift' to Google's Founders", 2018-04-24; [T2] What Matters, "What is an OKR?". Because OKRs express the outcome but not the causal chain, teams nest them beneath a metric tree [T2] North Star Framework, "OKRs vs KPIs vs roadmaps", §"OKRs vs North Star Framework".

**The Balanced Scorecard (BSC)** is the broadest hierarchy, spanning four perspectives — financial, customer, internal process, and innovation/learning — introduced by Kaplan and Norton in 1992 to counter over-reliance on financial accounting measures [T2] Kaplan & Norton, *Harvard Business Review*, Jan–Feb 1992, "The Balanced Scorecard—Measures that Drive Performance". Its *strategy map* links learning and capability objectives to processes, processes to customer value, and customer value to financial outcomes [T2] Kaplan, HBS Working Knowledge, "Partnering and the Balanced Scorecard", 2002-12-23, §"Figure 2-2". BSC observes the *capability* layer other frameworks ignore, though it can degrade into a leadership reporting model detached from execution [T2] North Star Framework, §"Balanced Scorecard vs North Star Framework".

**Financial decompositions** attach metrics to the capital-structure layer. DuPont decomposes ROE into net margin × asset turnover × equity multiplier, or the five-step version into tax burden × interest burden × EBIT margin × asset turnover × equity multiplier [T2] Investopedia, "DuPont Analysis", §"Formula and Calculation". Each factor is a lever; a rising equity multiplier is an accounting-leverage signal rather than proof of quality [T3] Investing With Purpose, https://iwpfinance.com/concepts/fundamental-analysis/dupont-5-step. Research extends DuPont into a risk tool, showing the margin factor captures how sales-growth volatility propagates into operating-profit volatility [T1] Nissim & Penman, "Profitability Decomposition", §2.1, ¶2. **Driver-based planning** generalises this into value-driver trees cascading from financial targets to operational levers, with KPMG describing frameworks four to five levels deep [T2] KPMG, "Driver-based planning", §"Advanced driver frameworks".

**Throughput accounting** attaches metrics to the *constraint* node: throughput T = sales − totally variable cost, operating expense OE, and inventory/investment I [T2] ACCA, "Throughput accounting and the theory of constraints", §"Throughput ratios". Goldratt's insight is that only improvements at the bottleneck raise throughput; an hour saved on a non-bottleneck is "a mirage" [T2] ACCA, "Throughput accounting", §"Throughput accounting". For a harness this argues against optimising every node: local efficiency at non-constraint nodes can increase inventory and reduce system throughput [T2] Barnard, "Overview of Throughput Accounting", §"The 75% rule".

## Growth accounting and unit-economics decompositions

The customer-transformation edge of the graph has its own additive decompositions. Revenue accounting expresses recurring revenue as `RR(t) = RNU(t) + RAU(t−1) + RRU(t) + ER(t) − RLU(t) − CR(t)`, separating new, retained, resurrected, expansion, lapsed, and contraction components [T2] Iyer, "User Growth and Revenue Measurement Fundamentals", 2020, §4.6. The SaaS **ARR bridge** is the operational form: beginning ARR + new + expansion − contraction − churned = ending ARR, with each component representing a different driver with different economics and predictability [T2] TMT Investment Banking Guide, "SaaS Financial Analysis: Key Schedules", 2026-03-05, §"The ARR bridge".

The key refinement is the split between **net revenue retention (NRR)** and **gross revenue retention (GRR)**. Because NRR's denominator is frozen at period start, its terms are additive: starting ARR plus expansion minus contraction minus churn. A rising NRR can therefore coexist with falling GRR — expansion paying for churn rather than churn improving — so both are carried as sibling nodes, and leaking new-logo revenue into the expansion column is the most common defect in a retention model [T3] KPI Tree (kpitree.io), https://kpitree.io/blog/net-revenue-retention-decomposition-kpi-tree.

**Cohort analysis** attaches these metrics to customer *vintages* rather than aggregate periods, because aggregate revenue can grow while successive cohorts generate lower ARPU and worse retention — buying "increasingly perishable revenue" [T3] Product Philosophy, https://productphilosophy.com/articles/cohort-based-unit-economics. Cohort waterfalls test management retention assumptions in diligence: if cohorts show NRR declining from 120% to 108% while the model assumes 115%, projections are likely too aggressive [T2] TMT Investment Banking Guide, §"The cohort revenue waterfall"; the same vintage decomposition isolates each cohort's contribution to new, expansion, and churn [T3] Pomegra, https://pomegra.io/learn/library/track-c-strategies/growth-investing/chapter-05-the-rule-of-40-and-saas-metrics/cohort-analysis.

## What is benchmarkable at each abstraction level

Benchmarkability falls off sharply from the financial layer to the operational layer. Financial data is richly benchmarkable because public filings are mandatory and machine-readable; unit-economics and operational layers are benchmarkable only through surveys, vendor telemetry, or registry data.

**Financial ratios and statements.** SEC EDGAR's XBRL APIs expose filing history and structured facts — the `companyfacts`, `companyconcept`, and `frames` endpoints — as unauthenticated JSON covering US-GAAP and IFRS taxonomies; `frames` returns one fact per reporting entity in a calendar period, enabling cross-company comparison [T2] SEC, "EDGAR Application Programming Interfaces (APIs)", §"XBRL Data APIs"; [T2] SEC EDGAR Data Frames API OpenAPI, "Frames" path description. For sector ratios, RMA's *Annual Statement Studies* publishes 22 classical ratios plus common-size line items across 750+ NAICS industries, arrayed by six asset and sales size categories [T2] RMA, "Annual Statement Studies: Financial Ratio Benchmarks", §"Financial Ratio Benchmarks includes". Damodaran's free sector datasets publish gross, operating, and net margins and return on capital by industry from January 2026 data [T2] Damodaran, NYU Stern, "Operating and Net Margins", 2026-01.

**Public-company panels.** Compustat North America provides US/Canadian fundamentals on 30,000+ active and inactive firms from 1950, CRSP provides price/return/volume data from 1925, and IBES provides analyst consensus forecasts and actuals from the mid-1970s; all are distributed through Wharton Research Data Services (WRDS) [T2] York University Libraries, "York Subscriptions – WRDS"; [T3] Institute for Automated Research, https://instituteforautomatedresearch.org/wiki/licensed/ibes/. Linking them requires bridge tables — the CRSP/Compustat Merged product is a separate subscription, and IBES must be linked via its own link rather than a naive CUSIP merge [T2] WRDS, "Linking CRSP with Compustat"; [T3] Institute for Automated Research.

**Private and registry data.** The UK Companies House API returns live data on limited companies free of charge, including officers and persons with significant control, with a free monthly bulk CSV snapshot and a streaming API for keeping a dataset current [T2] Companies House, "Companies House API" and "Companies House data products". It is one of the few free sources covering private firms, though financial detail is far thinner than US GAAP filings.

**Unit economics and operational metrics.** These rarely appear in filings. SaaS benchmarks come from voluntary surveys: KeyBanc & Sapphire's 2024 survey reported median ARR growth of 19%, gross dollar retention of 91%, net dollar retention of 101%, CAC payback of 20 months, and Rule of 40 of 15% [T2] KeyBanc Capital Markets & Sapphire Ventures, "2024 SaaS Survey", §"Key Performance Metrics". Benchmarkit reported expansion ARR reached 40% of total new ARR in 2024 and the New CAC Ratio rose 14% to a median of $2.00 of S&M per $1.00 of new ARR [T2] Benchmarkit, "2025 SaaS Performance Metrics", §"Customer Acquisition". OpenView/High Alpha's report, based on 800+ companies, tracks public-SaaS NRR stabilising around 110% and growth at 15% [T2] High Alpha & OpenView, "2024 SaaS Benchmarks Report", §"Median Growth and Net Revenue Retention". For e-commerce, metrics such as contribution margin, MER, AOV, and repeat rate are compiled by consultancies rather than filed [T3] Level CFO, https://levelcfo.com/benchmarks/ecommerce/; [T3] Ecom CFO, https://ecomcfo.co/2025-ecommerce-pl-benchmark-report/.

## Cost and licensing of benchmark datasets

Cost spans four orders of magnitude. At the free end, SEC EDGAR data is public domain, requires no key, and is subject to a fair-access policy limiting automated requests to 10 per second with a declared User-Agent containing contact information [T2] SEC, "Accessing EDGAR Data", §"Fair access"; [T2] SEC, "Developer Resources", §"Fair Access". Damodaran's aggregate sector datasets are free, but the company-level data behind them can no longer be redistributed because of vendor restrictions [T2] Damodaran, NYU Stern, "Useful Data Sets", §"About Data". Companies House public data is free via API and bulk download, though its legacy XML gateway search service charges £4.70 per month plus per-request charges [T2] GOV.UK, "Companies House data products", §"XML gateway search service".

At the paid end, Compustat, CRSP, and IBES are licensed annual subscriptions sold only through annual WRDS contracts (WRDS does not publish pricing and requires institutional contact, though it offers one-time custom pulls) [T2] WRDS, "What is WRDS?", §"WRDS Subscription Fees"; [T2] Florida State University Libraries, "For Faculty: Subscribing to New Datasets", §"Annual Subscriptions vs. One-Time Pulls". Practitioners place a CRSP + Compustat + mutual-fund-holdings bundle at roughly $100,000 per year for a university, WRDS often costlier than any single package [T3] XJMR forum, https://www.econjobrumors.com/topic/wrds-data-cost. RMA's *Annual Statement Studies* lists at $630 non-member and $430 member per edition [T3] ProSight Financial Association Shop, https://my.prosightfa.org/Shop/Statement-Studies. SaaS benchmark reports are typically free but lead-gated.

Licensing matters as much as price: the standard Compustat agreement grants a non-exclusive, non-transferable licence for use "within its own organization", forbids redistribution to third parties, and indexes renewal fees to CPI [T2] Standard & Poor's Compustat Subscription Agreement (UCSD license archive), §1–2. A harness must track which nodes depend on data that cannot be persisted, redistributed, or used outside the licensee organisation.

## Peer-matching methods

**Industry classification (SIC, NAICS, GICS)** groups firms by primary product market. The problems are well documented: mapping four-digit SIC to NAICS produced only 41.9% agreement, and GICS-to-SIC agreement is only about 56% [T1] Krishnan & Press 2003, *Contemporary Accounting Research* 20(4), §Abstract; [T1] Bhojraj et al. 2003, cited in Review of Accounting Studies 2025, §"Industry classification misfits", ¶3. Granularity is a trade-off: too fine yields too few firms dominated by outliers, too coarse makes peers heterogeneous, and valuation error is minimised at intermediate levels such as three-digit SIC or four/six-digit GICS [T1] Weiner, "The Impact of Industry Classification Schemes on Financial Research", §6, ¶5.

**Valuation-fundamental matching** selects peers by similarity in risk, profitability, growth, and cash flow rather than industry. Bhojraj and Lee developed a "warranted multiple" per firm and selected peers with the closest warranted multiple, finding sharp improvements over alternatives [T1] Bhojraj & Lee 2002, *Journal of Accounting Research*, §Abstract. Later work combining multiple valuation fundamentals produced the most accurate valuations for 81% of multiples tested, versus 19% for industry-based matching [T1] Nel, "An Optimal Peer Group Selection Strategy", §Abstract.

**Size and age matching** is increasingly used as a robustness filter. A 2025 simulation found that drawing peers from the largest firms in an industry, regardless of the treated firm's own size, vastly improved detection of misfits and core firms at the cost of a higher Type I error rate; accrual models perform poorly when the peer group comprises small firms [T1] Review of Accounting Studies, "Industry classification misfits", 2025-05-17, §"Results", ¶3. A systematic review concludes industry membership alone is insufficient: comparability is purpose-dependent, best assessed by combining industry classification with firm-specific, investor-based, product-market, and financial-statement similarity [T1] *Journal of Economic and Leadership*, "When Are Firms Truly Comparable?", 2025-09-30, §Abstract.

**Text- and search-based peers** avoid fixed taxonomies. Hoberg and Phillips' Text-based Network Industry Classifications (TNIC) derive product-market peers from 10-K product descriptions, evolve over time, and outperform SIC-3 and NAICS-4 at matching managers' self-reported rivals, with overlap rising to 52.2% at a 25% common-word threshold [T1] Hoberg & Phillips, "Text-Based Network Industries and Endogenous Product Differentiation", §5, ¶1. Separately, internet co-search behaviour aggregates investor perceptions into search-based peer groups [T1] Lee, Ma & Wang 2015, *Journal of Financial Economics* 116(2), §Abstract. Geographic and registry proxies (e.g., Companies House SIC plus region) are the fallback for private firms.

## Pitfalls of naive peer comparison

**Taxonomy dependence.** Because there is no universal industry structure, the classification scheme materially changes benchmark values and conclusions. One study showed a pharmaceutical company classified simultaneously as Manufacturing (NAICS), Health Care (GICS), and Life Sciences (SEC), each peer set implying different risk benchmarks [T1] Kasztelnik, "Hidden Handicaps of Benchmarking", §"Conclusion", ¶2. A benchmark is therefore conditional on its taxonomy and should be reported with the scheme and granularity that produced it.

**Finer is not automatically better.** Below a threshold, peer groups become too small and outlier-dominated; above it, too heterogeneous; valuation errors rise at both extremes and are minimised in the middle [T1] Weiner, §6, ¶5. Fixed schemes also lag reality, which is how hundreds of 1990s web firms ended up in a nondescript "business services" bucket [T1] Hoberg & Phillips, §"Introduction", ¶2.

**XBRL comparability erosion.** The machine-readable data that makes benchmarking cheap also degrades it. The SEC's 2009 XBRL mandate was associated with a decline in financial-statement comparability in its early years, particularly for firms using company-specific extension taxonomies [T1] Dhole, Lobo, Mishra & Pal 2015, *International Journal of Accounting Information Systems* 19, §Abstract. One analysis found 63% of data elements were not comparable between a pair of filings on average [T1] Zhu & Wu, "Interoperability of XBRL Financial Statements in the U.S.", 2011, §Abstract, and Debreceny et al. found 40% of extension elements were unnecessary because semantically equivalent standard elements existed [T1] Debreceny, Farewell, Piechocki, Felden, Gräning & D'Eri, "Flex or Break? Extensions in XBRL Disclosures to the SEC", 2011, §Synopsis. Quality improved after 2012 but weaknesses persisted in notes data and at smaller companies [T1] Markelevich, "The Quality and Usability of XBRL Filings in the US", 2016, §Abstract.

**Definition and denominator mismatch.** Benchmarks with identical names frequently measure different things. Public-SaaS CAC payback uses "net new implied ARR" in the denominator, while private-company CAC payback uses newly acquired contracted ARR — "NOT an apples to apples comparison" [T2] Benchmarkit, "2025 SaaS Performance Metrics", §"CAC Payback Period". Equal-weighting AOV across stores versus pooling total revenue produced a 1.9× difference on identical data ($83.27 versus $147.39) [T3] Top Growth Marketing, https://topgrowthmarketing.com/dtc-ecommerce-benchmarks/average-order-value-benchmarks/.

**Non-random, non-representative samples.** RMA states its data is not selected by any random or statistically reliable method, is categorised by primary product only, may contain small samples, and can be distorted by extreme statements, so it recommends using the figures only as general guidelines [T2] RMA, "Definition of Ratios", §"Recommended for Use as General Guidelines". Papers treating such samples as parametric populations draw over-confident inferences; formal comparison requires non-parametric testing because financial ratios are heavily skewed, heavy-tailed, and dependent [T1] Marozzi, "A Method to Address the Effectiveness of the SIC Code", §"Conclusions".

**Goodhart's law.** When a measure becomes a target it ceases to be a good measure; the robust defence is naming a counter-metric at the same moment as the target — pairing activation with early retention, delivery speed with change-failure rate [T3] Prakash Poudel Sharma. A tree that omits guardrail nodes invites optimising one branch at the expense of an adjacent one [T3] KPI Tree. Metric trees also rot: they encode a causal model that goes stale, so they need a revisit cadence permitted to declare the North Star itself wrong [T3] Prakash Poudel Sharma.

**Time and point-in-time bias.** Fundamental data must reflect only information available at each historical date; using fiscal quarter-end dates introduces look-ahead bias because filings arrive 30–60 days later, so the announcement date rather than the fiscal date is the correct knowledge time [T3] Stefan Jansen, machine-learning-for-trading, GitHub notebook, §"Point-in-Time (PIT) Correctness".

## Wiring benchmarks onto the business graph

Each node should carry a tuple: metric definition, owner, layer (financial / unit-economics / operational / capability), benchmark source, peer scheme and granularity, licence class, and a counter-metric. Place the financial decomposition (DuPont or driver tree) at the root, the most densely benchmarkable layer; attach unit-economics decompositions (ARR bridge, NRR/GRR, cohort ARPU) to customer-transformation edges, always carrying both gross and net retention; attach throughput metrics to the current bottleneck node rather than uniformly; and reserve capability metrics for the BSC layer, where first-party time series substitute for weak cross-company benchmarks. Treat every benchmark as taxonomy-conditional, denominator-specific, and sampling-biased: report the peer scheme with the number, and prefer peer selection combining industry, size, and valuation fundamentals.
